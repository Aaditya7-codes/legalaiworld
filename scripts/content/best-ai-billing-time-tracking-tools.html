The problem this category actually solves

Contemporaneous timekeeping is a well-known fiction. Most lawyers reconstruct their day at 6pm, or worse, on Friday — which means real work goes unbilled ("leakage"), narratives get generic and get bounced back by outside-counsel-guideline (OCG) reviews, and associates burn extra hours on an admin task nobody enjoys. AI time-tracking tools attack this by capturing activity as it happens — which app was open, which document was edited, which call ran how long — and drafting a billing entry automatically, so the lawyer is editing a draft instead of writing one from memory.

This is a genuinely different category from traditional timekeeping software (which just gives you a faster stopwatch and a form). The tools below use LLMs to read the content of what you did — not just which app had focus — and write a narrative a client or OCG reviewer would actually accept.

What "reads your screen" actually means (and the privacy question that follows)

Passive capture tools work by monitoring active application content — which document is open, what's in the email you're drafting, who's on the call — and using that to generate a plausible billing narrative. That's genuinely more accurate than metadata-only tracking (which only knows an app had focus, not what you did in it). It's also a legitimate confidentiality question under the same rules covered in ABA Formal Opinion 512 and state ethics guidance: before rolling this out, get a straight answer from the vendor on where captured content is stored, whether it's used to train models, and how long it's retained. A vendor that can't answer that clearly in writing is a pass, regardless of how good the product demo looks.

Buyer's guide by firm size

Choose Ajax if:

  • You want to see real pricing before a sales call.
  • You're a small-to-midsize firm that wants broad integration coverage without an enterprise procurement process.

Choose PointOne or Laurel if:

  • Your billing gets bounced by OCG reviewers regularly, and narrative quality/compliance is the actual pain point, not just capture.
  • You have the budget and scale to justify a custom deployment and dedicated onboarding.

Choose Clio Duo if:

  • You're already running Clio for practice management and don't want a second tool with its own login and data silo.
  • You're a small firm where "one more subscription" is a real objection, not a formality.

Choose Bill4Time if:

  • You want billing-first software without adopting a full practice-management platform.
  • Budget is the primary constraint and you don't need passive, content-aware capture.

Reality check: vendors in this category commonly advertise significant recovered-billable-time numbers (claims in the 30–50% range show up across several vendor blogs). Treat any specific percentage as a marketing claim to verify against your own trial data, not a guarantee — the honest version is "meaningfully less leakage than end-of-day manual entry," which is true across this whole category regardless of vendor.

Rollout checklist

  • Pilot with a small group first. Two to three timekeepers for 30 days, comparing AI-drafted entries against what they'd have logged manually.
  • Get the data policy in writing before the pilot, not after — see the confidentiality note above.
  • Check OCG compatibility if you do insurance defense or corporate-client work. Ask the vendor directly whether narrative output has been tested against major OCG formats, not just "clients love it."
  • Budget for edited-not-replaced workflows. Every tool in this category still expects a human to review and adjust the draft narrative — plan the rollout around that, not around full automation.

Conclusion

This category didn't really exist three years ago, and it's moved fast — PointOne alone reported 10x revenue growth in six months after a March 2026 Series A, which tells you where the market's headed. If you're evaluating for the first time: start with Ajax for a fast, low-commitment look at what content-aware capture feels like, use Clio Duo if procurement friction is the real obstacle, and go to PointOne or Laurel only once you know narrative quality and OCG compliance — not just capture — is the actual problem you're solving.
